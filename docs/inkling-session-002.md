# Session 002 · Does Inkling price a bluff?

Status — splits and training orders frozen. [Session 002 protocol and local baselines](../results/16-inkling-session-002/protocol.md). [Pre-training diagnostics](../results/15-inkling-audit/report.md). Parent — Session 001, step 140. The new experiment can use generated examples without another overnight gameplay run.

**Question.** With a losing hand, does Inkling risk a small bet yet decline a large one against the very same opponent? In the first local transfer check it got both decisions right on 7 of 12 pairs with hand strength given and 6 of 12 with cards alone. The plain practice quiz admitted a shortcut because 30 of 31 fold percentages predicted a single action on losing hands.

1. Generate independent pot sizes, bet sizes and fold probabilities. For each losing hand build a cheap and an expensive bluff around break-even. Include near-threshold decisions, several board textures and value hands. Use the chip formula to grade each choice. Exclude exact ties.
2. Keep matched pairs and related boards together when splitting training, development and fresh evaluation data. Keep Session 001’s sealed exam and reserved games out of development.
3. Freeze the new evaluation and score the original model and step 140 locally before training. Record the prompt, seed, decoding and hashes. Mix in earlier river and hand-reading examples during refinement.
4. Compare all three players on identical inputs. Count pairs where both actions are right, expected chips lost, profitable bluffs taken, bad bluffs taken, value bets and invalid answers. Report uncertainty over independent situations. Select only on development. Retain step 140 if the new method fails to improve fresh sizing decisions or sacrifices earlier skills.
5. Predeclare three independent training runs per method and matched evaluation seeds before spending. Record dataset shuffle, controllable initialization seed or provider limitation, parent path, rendering, checkpoint selection and cost for every run. Keep all outcomes. One run remains a pilot.
6. Keep the historical 1,908-row exam as a regression check. For release, freeze the candidate and its local runtime. Compare with both original and step 140 on the locked variable-price challenge (96 distinct fold rates, two bluff sizes and one value hand per group). Require lower mean decision loss than both, a positive paired 95% lower bound for improvement over step 140 across groups, and profitable-bluff and value-bet rates within five points of the better parent rate. Report pairs correct, invalid replies and hand-reading counterexamples separately. Run the normalized overlap guard against every training and development input before locking the model. Reserve the challenge's fold rates and related card deals from future training and development.
7. The 48 reserved fixed-price game seeds test whole-game behavior at a 50% threshold. Require paired game savings against the original with a 95% interval above zero, plus a separately preregistered variable-price game comparison against step 140 before claiming general game improvement. Match deals and opponent randomness; let each policy observe its own responses. Compare read calibration with a same-evidence counting learner and an observer given the simulator prior. Release and full-table trials remain separate decisions.
